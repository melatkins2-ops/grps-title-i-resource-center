Purchase Rationale Examples

Illustrative examples of stronger and weaker Title I purchase rationales.

Important: These examples demonstrate the level of specificity expected in a rationale. They do not establish allowability. Current MDE guidance and GRPS approval still control.

Example 1: Decodable readers

Weak:
We need new reading books for scholars.

Why it is weak: no identified need, no Title I strategy connection, no target group, and no explanation of how the materials will be used.

Stronger:
Grade 1–2 screening data show a significant group of scholars below benchmark in foundational reading skills. The school’s approved literacy strategy includes targeted small-group phonics intervention. The requested decodable readers will be used during those intervention blocks to provide controlled-text practice aligned to the skill sequence. Materials will be assigned to the intervention program, monitored through service schedules and progress data, and used only for the approved instructional purpose.

Example 2: Family workshop materials

Weak:
We want supplies and food for a parent night.
Stronger:
Family feedback and reading data indicate a need for caregivers to better understand the grade-level reading routines scholars are expected to practice at home. The proposed workshop will teach caregivers how to use the same fluency and comprehension prompts used in school. Requested take-home materials directly support that learning objective. Attendance, agenda, handouts, family feedback, and follow-up implementation will be retained as evidence.
