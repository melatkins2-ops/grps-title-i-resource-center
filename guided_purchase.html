Answer each condition

Answer each question with Yes, No, or Not sure. Click a selected response again to clear it and change your answer. The tool will tell you whether the request is ready, needs review, or should stop.

1. Is the need documented? The purchase is connected to the school/district needs assessment, MICIP, or approved program plan.
2. Is the cost supplemental? It is not replacing a service the district is otherwise required to provide.
3. Is the cost reasonable, necessary, and allocable? The cost is directly connected to the intended Title I beneficiaries and the approved purpose.
4. Can the required documentation be retained? Rationale, approval, invoice/receipt, coding, and implementation evidence can be maintained.

Common mistake: Do not start with the item you want to buy. Start with the identified need and the Title I purpose the cost is intended to support.
You are done when: You can explain the identified need, Title I purpose, why the cost is reasonable and necessary, what approval is required, and what documentation will be retained.
What happens next? If the request is ready, move it through the normal GRPS approval/purchasing process and retain the rationale and supporting documentation.

See 3 quick purchase examples

Reading software: Potentially allowable when tied to an identified instructional need, included in the Title I program, reasonable in cost, and properly approved/documented.

Family-night door prizes: Do not assume they are allowable because the event is Title I. The incentive itself needs a defensible program purpose and current guidance should be checked.

Conference travel: Start with the role of the attendee and how the learning supports the Title I-funded program—not simply that the conference is educational.
