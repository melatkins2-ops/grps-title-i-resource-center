What stronger implementation looks like

Learning-linked

Families leave with knowledge, strategies, or tools they can use to support scholar learning.

Two-way participation

Families have a meaningful role in planning, feedback, review, or decision-making—not only attendance.

Access is planned

Language, scheduling, accessibility, and participation barriers are considered in advance.

Input changes something

Family feedback is documented, reviewed, and connected to a response, adjustment, or next step.

Example

Too weak

Family fun night + sign-in sheet.

Stronger

Learning objective + family learning activity + access supports + participation evidence + family feedback + documented follow-up.

Design test: event or Title I family engagement?

Event-only design

Theme: “Family Fun Night”

Games, raffles, and food; no defined scholar-learning need; no specific caregiver learning outcome; success measured only by attendance.

Stronger Title I design

Need: K–2 reading data show scholars need additional fluency practice.

Caregiver outcome: families can explain and practice the school's fluency routine at home.

Activity: teacher models the routine, families practice with sample text, and families leave with a home-practice resource.

Access & evidence: flexible/repeated sessions, interpretation or other supports as needed, agenda, participation record, materials, family feedback, and follow-up.

Design standard: meaningful Title I family engagement is linked to learning, relational, two-way, accessible, and documented.
