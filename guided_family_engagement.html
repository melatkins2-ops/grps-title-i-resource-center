Build a Title I family engagement activity

Start by choosing the type of activity. The tool will narrow the questions that follow.

Families have a meaningful role in planning, input, or co-design—not only attendance.
The activity is linked to learning/school improvement rather than primarily entertainment or celebration.
Access barriers are addressed through flexible scheduling, communication, language access, transportation/childcare or other appropriate supports.
Two-way communication, family feedback, and follow-up are built into the activity.
The evidence plan includes agenda, sign-in/participation, materials, evaluation, and fiscal records when Title I funds are used.
Complete the required responses to unlock the design check.

Common mistake: A family event is not automatically Title I family engagement. Make the connection to scholar learning, meaningful family participation, and the identified need explicit.
You are done when: The activity has a clear learning connection, meaningful two-way family participation, access supports, and a plan for evidence and follow-up.
What happens next? After the activity, retain the agenda, participation evidence, materials, family feedback, and any follow-up or adjustment.

See weak vs. stronger family-engagement examples

Weak: “Family Fun Night” with games, food, and an attendance count, but no identified learning need or family learning outcome.

Stronger: A K–2 literacy evening tied to a fluency need where caregivers see a modeled routine, practice it, receive a take-home resource, give feedback, and the school retains the agenda, participation evidence, materials, and follow-up.
