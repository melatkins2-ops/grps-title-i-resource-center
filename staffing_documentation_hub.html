What stronger staffing documentation looks like

Role aligns to the approved program

The job description and actual assignment connect to approved Title I strategies/services.

Actual work is visible

Schedules, activity/time documentation, and service logs reflect what staff actually do.

Beneficiaries are clear

The district can show who received the service and how the funded role supported the intended Title I population.

Documentation is current

Records are completed on time and reviewed rather than reconstructed at year end.

Title I-funded staff should be able to show

Clear role

An approved job description or assignment that aligns to the Title I program and identified needs.

Appropriate duties

Work should be consistent with the funded position and should not create a disproportionate assignment of non-Title I duties.

Service evidence

Schedules, caseload/service logs, intervention records, instructional support, supervision records, or other evidence appropriate to the role.

Time/activity documentation

Maintain the time-and-effort or activity documentation required by the district and funding structure.

Funded-role alignment

The assignment, schedule, service, and documentation should all tell the same story about the funded purpose.

What should align

  • Approved role/job description
  • Actual assignment and schedule
  • Scholars/services supported
  • Activity/time documentation where required
  • Program strategy and budget
  • Implementation evidence

When to stop and resolve

  • Staff are regularly assigned unrelated duties
  • The schedule no longer matches the approved service
  • A funded position is vacant or materially changed
  • Documentation does not reflect actual work
  • The role has drifted from the program need
