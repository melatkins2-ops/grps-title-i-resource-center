Title I Essentials

The core ideas staff need in order to use the Resource Center well. Each concept routes directly to the workspace where the work happens.

Use this as orientation—not as a substitute for current guidance. Current federal/MDE requirements and current-year GRPS procedures control.

Title I is a program, not a purchase category

Start with the identified need and the Title I purpose. A purchase is not allowable simply because it is educational or useful. The cost must connect to the program design, be reasonable and necessary, and be supported by the appropriate approval and documentation.

Make a purchase decision →

Schoolwide does not mean unrestricted

A schoolwide program gives a Title I school flexibility to improve the whole educational program, but expenditures still must support the schoolwide plan/identified needs and meet federal cost principles and program requirements.

Connect planning & evaluation →

Family engagement is part of the program

Title I family engagement should be connected to scholar learning and meaningful two-way participation. The annual Title I meeting is one required component; it is not the entirety of family engagement.

Open Family Engagement →

Evidence should come from normal implementation

Strong documentation tells the story of why the work was selected, what was approved, what occurred, who participated, what happened, and what changed next. Evidence should be generated through the work—not assembled only when a review is approaching.

Build the evidence story →

Funded staff must match the funded purpose

Job descriptions, actual assignments, schedules, services delivered, salary charges, and time/effort documentation should align. Role drift should be identified and corrected rather than normalized.

Check staffing alignment →

Continuous improvement closes the loop

Title I implementation should connect need → strategy → implementation → participation → monitoring → decision → evaluation. The purpose is to improve the program, not create a parallel compliance process.

Open Monthly Title I Review →

Private-school services are an LEA responsibility

Equitable services require timely and meaningful consultation and an ongoing implementation process. The LEA maintains control of funds and Title I materials/equipment while services remain secular, neutral, and nonideological.

Open Private School Title I →

When unsure, route the question

Use the Find the Right Resource when you know the task but not the resource. Use Help & Contacts when a decision requires district interpretation or support.
