GRPS TITLE I RESOURCE CENTER

End-user pilot guide

Test whether staff can use the Resource Center independently to complete authentic Title I work.

Key rule: Do not teach participants how to use the site before the test. If the interface needs explanation, that is useful feedback.

5 minutes — Set up

Give the participant the Resource Center and ask them to choose a real Title I task they need to complete. Do not point them to a specific page.

15–25 minutes — Observe actual use

Watch where the participant clicks first, where they hesitate, words they do not understand, whether they backtrack, whether they can find examples or help, and whether they can identify a clear end point.

5–10 minutes — Gather feedback

Use the End-User Feedback page. Ask follow-up questions only to understand what happened; do not defend the design.

Who should test it?

Use a small cross-section before broad release: a principal/building leader, Title I-funded or program staff member, family-engagement staff member, fiscal/business staff member, central-office/state-and-federal staff member, and—if appropriate—a private-school/equitable-services user.

What counts as a serious issue?

Prioritize anything that prevents task completion, produces an incorrect compliance conclusion, requires verbal explanation to navigate, hides a required step, creates uncertainty about what to retain as evidence, or fails on a phone/smaller screen.

What not to overreact to

Do not redesign because one user prefers a different color, wording style, or page order unless the issue affects comprehension, accessibility, accuracy, or task completion. Look for patterns across users.
